# Idea 005: Cross-Team Discovery — Validating Platform Demand

## Raw Dump

This is discovery, not planning. Wave #224847. Audience: Product Leadership, Executive Stakeholders.

Purpose: validate platform use cases, define shared vs. domain-specific boundaries, build a demand-driven roadmap for Action Engine adoption across GPN.

Why: Genius Restaurant validates the technology works. Discovery validates that OTHER product teams have use cases worth building for, and that the infrastructure is genuinely reusable vs. overfit to one vertical.

Goals:
- Validate the platform thesis: do other teams have real use cases that map to Action Engine's capabilities (current or near-term)?
- Define platform boundary: what's shared infrastructure vs. domain-specific customization?
- Sequence the roadmap: based on actual demand, data readiness, and strategic value — which teams and use cases come first?
- Build organizational alignment: every discovery call is also a stakeholder alignment opportunity. Teams should leave feeling heard and clear on what Action Engine is (and isn't).

Process — three-phase cycle, same for every team:
Phase 1 (Prep): Pre-populate team profile with known context. Send invite with agenda and lightweight framing. No heavy pre-work from the team.
Phase 2 (Discovery Call): Structured 45-min. Action Engine overview → merchant pain points → collaborative use case mapping in Lucid → next steps.
Phase 3 (Follow-Up): Summary within 48 hours. Use cases logged in registry. Patterns synthesized after every 3-4 calls.

Meeting framing (from the invite/agenda):
"You've been identified as a Product person who may have merchant pain points, relevant roadmap items, or potential use cases for the Action Engine... This is discovery, not planning. No prep needed. Come ready to talk about and advocate for your merchants."

What feeds out:
- Use Case Registry: every use case classified by agent type, data readiness, strategic value, shared vs. domain-specific. This is the pipeline feeding roadmap sequencing.
- Pattern Synthesis: after every 3-4 calls, cross-team patterns identified. Multiple teams wanting the same capability = validate as shared infrastructure. Gaps across teams = signal for DLAK/data governance investment.
- Demand signal for leadership: real picture of internal demand, team readiness, and blockers. Informs resourcing, prioritization, and the managed service → co-development → self-service evolution pace.

Important: discovery also surfaces the data readiness picture. If multiple teams want similar capabilities but their data isn't ready, that's a DLAK and data governance investment signal — not just an Action Engine problem.

## Essence
A structured discovery process for validating Action Engine platform demand across GPN product teams. Three-phase cycle (prep → 45-min call → 48-hour follow-up), same format for every team. Outputs feed into a Use Case Registry, cross-team pattern synthesis, and a leadership-facing demand signal. The explicit framing — "this is discovery, not planning" — keeps teams from over-indexing on timelines before feasibility is validated.

## Interesting Because
The distinction between "discovery" and "planning" is load-bearing here. Setting that expectation explicitly in the invite is smart — it lowers the stakes for the team being interviewed (no deliverables, no commitments), which probably gets you more honest information than if they thought they were signing up for a roadmap dependency.

The pattern synthesis cadence (every 3-4 calls) is interesting. You're essentially running a lightweight qualitative research synthesis process inside a corporate stakeholder engagement. The Use Case Registry is the structured output, but the pattern synthesis is where the real platform intelligence comes from.

Also: "if multiple teams want similar capabilities but their data isn't ready, that's a signal for DLAK and data governance investment, not just an Action Engine problem." That sentence reframes data readiness gaps as organizational investment signals rather than blockers. That's a good reframe for exec conversations.

## Expansion Seeds
- The Use Case Registry is the most underrated artifact here. If built well, it becomes the living roadmap that updates itself as teams cycle through discovery.
- The 48-hour follow-up summary is a forcing function for synthesis. Worth being explicit about who writes it, what format it takes, and where it lives — that's where consistency either holds or breaks.
- "Teams should leave feeling heard and clear on what Action Engine is (and isn't)" — the "isn't" part is as important as the "is." Worth being really crisp on what not to promise in these calls.
