**When the merge queue gets long, 3 to 6 PRs from one part of the code can now land as one wave PR: one /ship, one READY, one train slot and one version.** Every slot in a train pays for its own stamp commit, its own round of about 12 required workflow runs (about 15 minutes, one slot after another) and its own version. The last 10 merged trains landed 26 PRs this way, with 16 to 24 READY PRs waiting at every build (refs/notes/gstack-trains; gate runs 41165841909 to 41217563527). Garry's rule R46 (2026-10-31) asks every Captain to group functional areas into wave PRs when queues get long. `gstack-ship-queue wave` now does this inside gstack's own train tooling, so crews get it from gstack instead of from prose (GST-574).

### Added
- **`gstack-ship-queue wave plan`** (read-only) counts the live READY PRs (open, head equal to their READY receipt). Over 6, it prints `WAVE_ADVISED` and suggested groups of 3 to 6. PRs that change a common file always share a group. Each group takes its largest PR's area (a skill directory, the first word of a `lib/`, `bin/` or `test/` module name, or `docs`), and its integrator is the PR with the most changed lines. `train build` prints the same `WAVE_ADVISED` line when handed more than 6 pins. It is advice, never a refusal.
- **`wave build --prs <n>@<sha>,...`** makes the integrator branch `wave/<nonce>`. It merges each constituent's pinned head onto main with the train's merge rules, as a merge commit whose second parent is the pin, so no constituent is rebased or squashed. It writes the wave record to `refs/notes/gstack-trains` before pushing and leaves one "frozen in wave" comment on each constituent. A conflict stops with `QUEUE_WAVE_CONFLICT` and leaves the merge in a worktree under the state root. `--continue` takes the integrator's committed resolution only when that commit merges exactly that pin. `--drop <pr>` rebuilds the wave without one PR on a new branch (never a force-push), and refuses with `QUEUE_WAVE_DEPENDENT` when another pin contains the dropped one.
- **`ready` and `train build` check a wave PR before it gets a slot.** They walk the wave branch back to its recorded base: every pin must be merged unchanged and in order, any other merge is refused, and every constituent must still be open at its pin. Otherwise they refuse with `QUEUE_WAVE_MOVED`. They refuse with `QUEUE_WAVE_UNKNOWN` when a `wave/` branch has no record, and with `QUEUE_WAVE_DUPLICATE` when a PR is pinned both on its own and inside a wave. READY gains `WAVE:` and `CONSTITUENTS:` lines. After that the wave PR is an ordinary slot: `train land`, `verify`, the stamp and the backstop are unchanged.
- **`wave settle --wave <nonce>`** runs after the wave PR merges. It checks that main's merge carries the wave head's tree. Each constituent still at its pin gets a "Landed in wave #W as vX" comment, naming any integrator commits that touched its files, and is closed. A constituent whose head moved stays open with a comment saying its newer work did not land. Reruns post nothing twice, and `--dry-run` shows what it would do.
- `docs/ship-policy.md#wave-prs`, the merge-train section of `docs/captain.md`, the `/captain` skill, the READY template's `WAVE` lines and a `WAVE_TRIGGER` crew parameter. Seven new result codes, each with a troubleshooting entry.

### Tests
- `test/ship-queue-wave.test.ts` runs the real bin against the train fixture. It covers area and grouping rules; a wave build's merge chain, record, push and notices; size and moved refusals; a conflict, an early `--continue` refused, then a resolution; `--drop` as a new wave, and its dependent refusal; `ready` and `train build` reading a wave from a record rebuilt from notes, with moved, duplicate and unknown refusals; `WAVE_ADVISED` and `wave plan`; and `wave settle` closing, leaving open and staying idempotent.
